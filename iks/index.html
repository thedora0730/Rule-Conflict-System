Śāstra Rule Precedence

Every conflict is resolved according to a five-tier hierarchy inherited from classical Śāstra interpretive method (Mīmāṃsā Nyāya).

I

Emergency

Ā­patkālika rules — invoked only under crisis or exceptional risk to life, safety, or dharma. Always overrides every other tier.

↓
II

Exception

Apavāda rules — carve out a deliberate exception to a general rule (Utsarga). An apavāda always defeats the utsarga it qualifies.

↓
III

Specific

Viśeṣa rules — apply to a narrower, more particular context. Under the maxim viśeṣo vidhīyate, the specific rule prevails over the general.

↓
IV

General

Sāmānya rules — broadly applicable rules of wide scope, applied whenever no specific or exception rule intervenes.

↓
V

Default

Anuvāda / baseline rules — fallback provisions applied only when no higher-tier rule is triggered.

About This Project

Bridging classical Indian epistemology with modern rule-based reasoning systems.

Indian Knowledge Systems (IKS)

Indian Knowledge Systems (IKS) refer to the vast body of traditional Indian knowledge spanning philosophy, logic, linguistics, astronomy, medicine, and ritual science, developed and refined over millennia through the Śāstra (treatise) tradition. IKS is not a single discipline but an interconnected ecosystem where rules, commentaries, and interpretive principles govern how texts are read and how conflicting instructions are resolved.

Śāstra Rule Precedence

Classical Mīmāṃsā hermeneutics developed a systematic method for resolving contradictions between scriptural injunctions. When two rules apply to the same situation but demand different actions, precedence is determined by well-defined interpretive maxims — an exception (apavāda) overrides a general rule (utsarga), a specific rule (viśeṣa) overrides a general one (sāmānya), and emergency or crisis provisions (āpatkālika) override all else. This system directly parallels rule-precedence and conflict-resolution strategies used in modern expert systems and business rule engines.

Importance of Rule Conflict Detection

As rule-based systems scale — whether in legal codes, medical protocols, ritual calendars, or software business logic — the number of rules grows and so does the likelihood of silent contradictions. Undetected conflicts lead to unpredictable or incorrect system behavior. A formal conflict detection layer, paired with a transparent precedence mechanism, ensures that every decision the system makes can be traced, justified, and trusted.

Applications

This precedence-based reasoning model is applicable well beyond Śāstric texts: in legal rule engines that reconcile statutes and exceptions, in clinical decision-support systems weighing general protocols against patient-specific exceptions, in compliance and policy engines, and in any expert system where multiple production rules can fire on the same input.

Research Motivation

Most conventional rule engines resolve conflicts using arbitrary tie-breaking such as rule order or recency, offering little interpretability. This project explores whether the centuries-old, well-formalized precedence logic of Śāstra hermeneutics can offer a more principled, explainable alternative — one where every resolution is accompanied by a stated reason drawn from a recognized interpretive maxim.

Future Scope

Planned extensions include support for weighted multi-rule conflicts, integration with a natural-language rule authoring interface, a persistent backend for multi-user rule bases, and a formal mapping layer connecting each precedence maxim to its original Sanskrit source citation.

References

  • Jaimini, Mīmāṃsā Sūtra — foundational text on Vedic hermeneutics and interpretive maxims.
  • Kumārila Bhaṭṭa, Tantravārttika — commentary elaborating utsarga–apavāda and viśeṣa–sāmānya principles.
  • Coursework and lecture material on Nyāya-based logical reasoning frameworks.
  • Contemporary literature on rule-based expert systems and conflict-resolution strategies (Rete algorithm, priority-based firing).
